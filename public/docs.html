Primary journey: connected screen flow

The recommended journey runs from banner to payment with no dead ends. Adding pizza 1 moves the customer straight on to the AED 1 selection. The confirmation and the unlock happen on that same screen, so there's no pop-up and no extra tap. Click any screen to open it in the prototype.

Recovery journey: when pizza 2 is skipped

If the customer leaves after pizza 1, the unclaimed offer surfaces again at every later touchpoint: the home banner, the cart and checkout. Each one is a single tap back to Step 2, and none of them blocks the order.

Three approaches, compared

Each approach was scored for a portrait touchscreen with a queue behind the customer. Tap counts cover the happy path from the banner to the checkout button, with no optional customization.

Recommendation: Hybrid A + B, "Guided journey with cart recovery"

Use Approach A's linear, auto-advancing journey as the main path, and add Approach B's cart offer card as a safety net. One pricing engine connects them and re-evaluates the offer on every cart change.

  • Zero discovery cost: adding pizza 1 lands the customer on the AED 1 selection, so nobody has to find the offer in the cart.
  • One screen per decision: each customization gets the full screen height, which suits kiosk viewing distance, unlike Approach C's cramped dual builder.
  • Never lost: skipping, exiting or going straight to the cart leaves the offer in a "waiting" state. The home banner, the cart card and a checkout strip each bring the customer back with one tap.
  • No coupon codes, no surprise pop-ups: the discount appears as a struck-through price at every step, and a green savings row in the cart.
  • Persistent mini-cart: on browsing screens, the item count, total and savings are always visible at the bottom.

Key UX decisions

Promotional pricing & eligibility model

The model avoids inventing restrictions. Every rule that isn't stated in the offer copy ("Buy 1 large or medium pizza and get 2nd Pizza for 1 AED") is a configurable assumption, and the prototype's left panel runs each one live.

Offer state machine

The cart is always in exactly one of these states. Each state has one UI treatment, which is reused on the home banner, the cart card, the checkout strip and the mini-cart.

Not startedNo eligible pizza in the cart. The banner shows the standard offer artwork.
Unlocked · waitingA qualifying pizza is in the cart and the AED 1 pizza isn't chosen yet. Shows the "Your AED 1 pizza is waiting" card and banner, plus a reminder strip at checkout.
AppliedAn AED 1 pizza is paired with its qualifier. Shows the AED 1 badge, the struck-through price and the green savings row.
On holdAn AED 1 pizza has lost its eligibility (qualifier removed, size rule, crust rule). It's charged at regular price, and a one-tap fix explains why.
Upgrade nudgeOnly small pizzas are in the cart. Offers a one-tap upgrade to medium that unlocks the deal.

Open questions for the business

  1. Can the AED 1 pizza be a larger size than the first pizza? (Default: yes, since the offer copy doesn't restrict it. A toggle exists.)
  2. Are premium crusts allowed on the AED 1 pizza, and if so, at the normal upcharge? (Default: allowed and charged.)
  3. How many AED 1 pizzas are allowed per order (1, one per qualifying pizza, unlimited)? (Default: 1.)
  4. When two eligible pizzas are added through the regular menu, should the offer auto-apply? If so, to the lowest-priced pizza? (Default: yes, to the lowest-priced.)
  5. Can it be combined with other coupons or loyalty rewards? Which stores, channels and dates? (Not modeled.)
  6. Are any pizzas excluded (for example, premium recipes)? The eligible list is a data flag on each product.
